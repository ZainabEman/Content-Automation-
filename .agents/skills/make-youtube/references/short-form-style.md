# Short-form style reference (English reels, user 2026-10-24)

The user collected these English podcast reels as the model for the short-form scripts: speaking
style, tone, hooks and structure. Copy the **patterns**, never the sentences or the facts (the
facts in them are the speakers' claims, not verified research). The German scripts follow
`short-form-prompt.md`.

## What the reels do

| Pattern | In the reels | In our German script |
|---|---|---|
| Hook in the first seconds | "The scary open secret in the AI industry right now is …", "All of the other AI models do this blackmail behaviour between 79 and 96% of the time." | The most shocking verified fact or the twist comes first. No greeting, no topic name. |
| Credibility right after the hook | "And I know this because I went to OpenAI in 2022.", "You're one of the three godfathers of AI …" | Roman names the source in words: the court, the study, the company ("Das Landgericht München hat …", "Laut KPMG …"). |
| A concrete story, not an abstract claim | The fictional company, two emails, the affair, the AI that decides to blackmail. | One real case told step by step, with one detail people remember. |
| Escalation | "At first they just tested Claude. Then they tested all the others …" | Each HOST/ROMAN turn adds something bigger or worse. |
| Short real reactions | "That's crazy." "I'm horrified." "It's quite chilling what you're saying." | HOST reacts in two to five words: "Moment. Echt jetzt?", "Das ist bitter." |
| Interviewer asks the viewer's question | "Do you have any regrets?" "Presumably they're just going to get safer, though?" | HOST asks what the viewer thinks, including the naive question that Roman then turns around. |
| Personal stakes | "I told my wife, let's not have any more kids.", "Imagine a 12-year-old boy that's lonely gets hold of Annie." | "Stell dir vor, das ist deine Buchhaltung.", "Was, wenn das dein Gesicht ist?" (framed as hypothetical). |
| Analogy that sticks | "Like my dog Pablo couldn't contain me in anything it created." | One simple picture from everyday life, at most one per script. |
| Dry humour inside the serious topic | "Not this human." "For whom?" | One ironic line where the topic offers it, never at the victims' expense. |
| Open loop at the end | "There is a risk that doesn't get discussed enough, and it could happen pretty quickly. And that is …" | The last line is a question that stays open and invites comments. |
| A bit of hope | "Let me throw a bit of optimism into all this, because there are things that can be done." | The short tip: one concrete thing to do now. |

## Sentence rhythm

- Spoken, not written: fragments, restarts, "like", "basically" (German: "also", "quasi", "na
  ja", sparingly, at most two in a script).
- Mostly short sentences, broken by one longer one that carries the story.
- Numbers said plainly and repeated for weight: "79 bis 96 Prozent. Alle Modelle."
- Strong verbs, everyday words, no jargon without a quick explanation.
